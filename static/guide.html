WORK WITH WHAT REMAINS

The field guide.

SecondCut is a research prototype for recovering one flat rectangular cardboard blank and generating a compatible two-panel cross-slot organizer insert. It does not reconstruct arbitrary objects or certify structural strength.

1. Prepare the photograph

  1. Download the A4 mat. Print at actual size (210 × 297 mm), without “fit to page.” Verify the horizontal distance between the top marker centers is 180 mm and the vertical distance between left marker centers is 267 mm.
  2. Use a matte brown cardboard blank on the white mat. Keep it flat, horizontal, and inside the central area, below the top markers and above the bottom markers. Printed graphics, pale cardboard, strong shadows, and gloss are unsupported.
  3. Photograph all four markers and every edge of the blank. Move close enough to keep the markers sharp. Do not cover holes or damaged areas.
  4. Enter measured thickness and your minimum acceptable dimensions. Dimensions are in millimetres. The solver reserves 2 mm around all observed edges and holes.

2. Review the repair

The green rectangle shows the blank that can be cut out of the remaining material. Options cannot exceed the original design dimensions. A smaller main panel changes the insert footprint; check it still suits your intended container. Retained area refers only to the main panel, not total project material or carbon savings.

The companion panel is generated from separate stock. Both panels have the same height; the main panel has a slot from the top, and its companion has a slot from the bottom. Slot widths use your entered thickness. Test the fit on spare material first.

3. Cut and check

Accept a plan to unlock its SVG. Print at 100% and verify the 50 mm scale bar. Align the main outline with the photographed recovery area, then transfer it to the cardboard. The tool does not control cutting equipment. Use appropriate hand tools and supervision.

Photograph the trimmed, slotted main panel on the mat. Verification compares width, height, silhouette, and whether the slot is actually open. The tolerance is ±3 mm with silhouette overlap of at least 94% and slot clearance of at least 75%. These are prototype thresholds, not validated accuracy guarantees. Check both panels fit together by hand.

What the samples prove

All built-in sample images are generated, including the repaired-panel example. They exercise the actual OpenCV and planning code. They do not establish real-world camera accuracy, usability, strength, or material savings. Physical validation is pending.

Data and decisions

Inspection evidence is stored in this deployment’s local database. Images are not sent to a model provider. The workflow uses deterministic tool orchestration and a geometric solver, not an LLM. It chooses recapture, recovery review, or replacement based on visual evidence. Exported records include input provenance and tool decisions.
